# Brackets — Design Context

*This file is loaded automatically by `/impeccable` skills (craft, polish, critique, etc.). It defines the design direction for the project so every subsequent design pass has strong, specific guidance instead of defaulting to generic AI aesthetics.*

---

## Design Context

### Users

A group of **30-something friends** playing together on a couch. The host plugs a PC into the TV to broadcast the full bracket, host controls, and round-level drama; every other player joins from their **smartphone** to propose candidates and vote.

The actual product isn't the UI — it's the **argument**. Voting unleashes heated discussion about pizza toppings, Disney films, best Wes Anderson shot, etc. The interface is a stage for that argument. Success = the group keeps talking and laughing, not "the app was efficient."

Two viewing contexts must be visually distinct:

- **Host / TV view** — broadcast. Large type, scoreboard energy, readable across the room.
- **Player / phone view** — contestant's podium. Focused, tactile, one action at a time.

### Brand Personality

**Competitive · sporting · tabloid** — editorial credibility meets sports-page theatre.

Think *Sunday Times* or *La Gazzetta dello Sport* running a bracket on "best gelato flavor" with a straight face. The gravitas of a real publication making a real ruling, applied to wonderfully stupid subjects.

Tone guardrails:

- **Confident, not snarky.** The UI never winks at the user. The *topic* is silly; the *treatment* is serious. The humor comes from the contrast.
- **Theatrical, not kitsch.** Scoreline typography and VS moments are staged with intention, not retro-TV cheese.
- **Editorial fundamentals underneath.** Real hierarchy, real leading, real grids. Sports-paper energy sits on top of a properly-designed page, not in place of one.

Emotional goals: anticipation before a reveal, authority during a result, room-filling volume at round transitions.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme**: Light. Newsprint / off-white paper — a warm, slightly tinted background (NOT pure `#fff`). Dark ink sits on paper the way a real broadsheet looks.

**Primary accent**: **Blue**. A printing-ink blue with real saturation — not indigo, not cyan, not the `#6366f1` currently in the codebase. Something closer to a classical sports-page ink or a passport stamp. Use OKLCH and tint neutrals very slightly toward this blue for cohesion.

**Secondary accent**: A warm sports-paper red or vermillion, used RARELY — for live state ("VOTING"), eliminated sides, or round-end drama. Never as a sole information carrier (see accessibility below).

**Typography direction** (final faces to be chosen during `/impeccable craft`, but NOT from the reflex-reject list; specifically NOT Inter, DM Sans, Fraunces, Playfair, IBM Plex, Space Grotesk, etc.):

- **Display** — a condensed editorial/sports face for round titles, VS, scorelines, and the champion reveal. Must feel like it could appear above a match report.
- **Body / UI** — a workhorse text face that carries Italian and English equally well. Humanist sans or text serif with real Italian accent support.
- **Numerals** — tabular, prominent. Vote counts and round numbers are *display elements*, not captions.

**Anti-references** (things to not look like):

- The current style (indigo glow, 12px rounded cards, Inter, generic dark-mode dashboard)
- Generic SaaS dashboards / Linear clones
- Crypto-style neon-on-black "sleek" aesthetics
- Childish bracket-maker apps (Challonge, generic tournament sites)
- Gradient-text headers, glassmorphism, side-stripe alert cards, sparkline decoration

### Design Principles

1. **Theatre over utility.** Design for the room and the argument, not for task completion. The host screen is a broadcast; the player screen is a stage door.

2. **Two dramaturgies, one system.** Host-on-TV and player-on-phone share the same type and color system but have fundamentally different layouts. Phone-first does NOT mean "shrink the TV view."

3. **Print credibility, stadium energy.** Sports-page intensity (condensed headlines, VS as an event, ceremonial round labels) built on editorial fundamentals (hierarchy, leading, grids, generous whitespace).

4. **Deuteranopia-safe by default.** Red and green must never be the sole distinguisher of state. Pair every color signal with weight, position, shape, icon, or text. Test the palette under a deuteranopia simulator before shipping any round of changes.

5. **Bilingual-aware.** Every string lives in both Italian and English. Type scales, line heights, and layouts must survive the longer words ("Quarti di finale" vs "Quarter Final", "Pareggio" vs "Tie"). No hardcoded widths. No fixed truncation that only works in one language.

6. **No reflex defaults.** No Inter. No indigo. No 12px rounded cards with drop shadows. No gradient text. No `border-left` accent stripes. Every choice is picked deliberately for *this* product's specific staging.
